### 1. About Knowledge Distillation (15%)

- **What Modes of Distillation is used in this Lab?**
  In this lab, we used **Offline Distillation**. We first obtained a pre-trained Teacher model (ResNet50), froze its parameters (set to eval mode), and then used it to guide the training of the Student model (ResNet18).

- **What role do logits play in knowledge distillation? What effect does a higher temperature parameter have on logits conversion ?**

  - **Role:** Logits (the raw output before Softmax) carry richer information about the relationships between classes than hard labels (0 or 1). They represent the "dark knowledge" of the model—for example, indicating that a specific image of a "cat" looks 10% like a "dog" but 0% like a "plane".

  - **Effect of Temperature ($T$):** A higher Temperature parameter softens the probability distribution generated by Softmax ($q_i = \frac{\exp(z_i/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)}$). It prevents the distribution from collapsing into a peak (resembling a one-hot vector), thereby revealing the subtle inter-class similarities hidden in the smaller logit values.

- **In Feature-Based Knowledge Distillation, from which parts of the Teacher model do we extract features for knowledge transfer?**

  We extract feature maps from the **intermediate hidden layers**. Specifically, in the ResNet architecture, we return the outputs from the four main stages (blocks): `[feature1, feature2, feature3, feature4]`, which correspond to the outputs of `layer1` through `layer4`.

### 2. Response-Based KD (30%)

Please explain the following:

- **How you choose the Temperature and alpha?**

  - **Temperature ($T = 4.0$):** We chose 4.0 to sufficiently soften the teacher's logits, making the distribution smoother and allowing the student to learn the structural relationships between classes without the distribution becoming too flat (uniform).
  - **Alpha ($\alpha = 0.9$):** We set a high alpha (0.9) to give more weight to the distillation loss (KL Divergence) than the standard Cross-Entropy loss. This forces the student to prioritize mimicking the teacher's behavior over just learning the hard ground truth labels.

- **How you design the loss function?**
  The total loss is a weighted sum of the standard classification loss and the distillation loss:
  $$L_{total} = (1 - \alpha) \times L_{CE}(y_{student}, y_{true}) + \alpha \times T^2 \times L_{KL}(\sigma(\frac{z_{student}}{T}), \sigma(\frac{z_{teacher}}{T}))$$
  - We used `nn.CrossEntropyLoss` for the hard targets (Student vs. Label).
  - We used `nn.KLDivLoss` for the soft targets (Student Logits vs. Teacher Logits), scaling by $T^2$ to ensure the gradient magnitudes remain comparable to the hard target loss.

### 3. Feature-based KD (30%)

Please explain the following:

- **How you extract features from the choosing intermediate layers?**
  We modified the `ResNet.forward()` method to return not just the final logits but also a list of feature maps `[f1, f2, f3, f4]` generated by the four residual layers.
  Since the Teacher (ResNet50, Bottleneck) has 4x the number of channels compared to the Student (ResNet18, BasicBlock) at each stage (e.g., 256 vs 64 at layer 1), we implemented **1x1 Convolutional Regressors** in the `Distiller` class. These layers project the Student's feature dimensions to match the Teacher's dimensions before calculating the loss.

- **How you design the loss function?**
  We adopted a Mean Squared Error (MSE) approach to align the feature maps:
  - We iterated through the four pairs of feature maps (Student's projected feature vs. Teacher's feature).
  - We calculated `F.mse_loss` for each pair.
  - The final loss is the summation of these MSE losses added to the standard Cross-Entropy loss: $L_{total} = L_{CE} + \beta \sum L_{MSE}(f_{student}, f_{teacher})$.

### 6. Implementation Observations and Analysis (20%)

Based on the comparison results above:

**Did any KD method perform unexpectedly?**
Yes, two notable observations occurred:

1.  **Teacher Performance:** The Teacher model (ResNet50) achieved lower accuracy (86.03%) than the Student model trained from scratch (86.88%).
2.  **Feature-based Loss:** The Feature-based student had a significantly higher loss (3.17) compared to other models, yet its accuracy did not improve (86.72% vs 86.88%).

**What do you think are the reasons?**

1.  **Teacher < Student:** ResNet50 is a much deeper and complex model than ResNet18. On the CIFAR-10 dataset (small 32x32 images), ResNet50 is prone to **overfitting** or may require more careful hyperparameter tuning/augmentation and more epochs to converge compared to the lighter ResNet18. Since the teacher wasn't "smarter," the upper bound for knowledge transfer was limited.

2.  **Feature-based Issues:** The loss of 3.17 indicates that the **MSE Loss from feature matching dominated the total loss**. The values in feature maps can be large, leading to a high MSE. Since we used a weight of $\beta=1.0$, the optimizer focused heavily on mimicking feature maps rather than classifying images correctly. This distraction prevented the model from improving its accuracy. To solve this, the weight ($\beta$) should be reduced (e.g., to 0.05) to balance the task loss and distillation loss.

**Observations during implementation:**

- **Response-based Success:** Even though the Teacher had lower accuracy, Response-based KD still improved the Student's performance (86.88% -> 87.43%). This suggests that the soft targets acted as a **regularizer**, preventing the student from becoming overconfident and helping it generalize better, regardless of the teacher's absolute accuracy.
- **Technical Difficulty (RuntimeError):** During the implementation of Feature-based KD, I encountered a `RuntimeError: Input type (torch.cuda.FloatTensor) and weight type (torch.FloatTensor) should be the same`. This happened because the new regressor layers defined in `Distiller.__init__` were initialized on the CPU while the input data was on the GPU. I solved this by ensuring `distiller.to(device)` was called before training, which moved all sub-modules (including the new regressors) to the GPU.

- **Critical Comparison of Feature Loss Weights (β):**

I conducted a comparative analysis between two different weight settings for the feature-based loss.

1. **Experiment A (β=1.0 epochs=50) original:** The total loss was extremely high (~3.17) due to the magnitude of MSE. The student failed to outperform the baseline, indicating the optimizer was "distracted" by strictly forcing feature alignment at the cost of classification accuracy.

2. **Experiment B (β=0.05 epochs=20):** By significantly reducing the weight, the total loss stabilized at a normal range (0.65). Crucially, this setting unlocked the potential of distillation: the Feature-based student (86.21%) successfully surpassed both the Student baseline (83.71%) and the Teacher (82.32%). This comparison proves that feature matching is most effective when treated as a soft auxiliary task. A smaller β allows the model to benefit from the teacher's feature hints without being overwhelmed by them.
